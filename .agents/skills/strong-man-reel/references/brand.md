## 1. The page

- **What it is:** a Facebook page for men that teaches them how to become strong men in mind, body,
  money and family. Name: The Strong Man Code (alternatives: Strong Man Rules, Weak Man Strong Man).
- **Audience:** men aged 18–45 in the UK and the USA: students, young workers, husbands and fathers
  who feel stuck and want to level up.
- **Goal:** original content that gets shares and saves, qualifies for Facebook Content Monetization,
  and later sends followers to a digital products store.
- **Language:** simple, clear English that works in both the UK and the USA. Short sentences. No slang
  that only one country uses. US spelling.

## 2. Voice and tone

The page speaks as a **wise, charismatic older man** who has already made the mistakes and now speaks
with authority. He talks down from experience, not from arrogance: calm, direct, certain and a little
cold. He challenges the viewer but never mocks or insults him.

**Do:** short, heavy sentences. Speak to "you". State hard truths as facts. Pause for effect. End with
a clear rule or order.

**Don't:** jokes, emojis, slang, begging for likes, shouting, insulting the viewer, putting down women
or any group.

Example lines (the register to match):
- "Most men will scroll past this. That's why most men stay the same."
- "Discipline is not a mood. It's a decision you make before the mood arrives."
- "You don't need more motivation. You need fewer excuses."

## 3. Signature device: weak man vs strong man

Use it when the topic fits, not in every reel. Many reels compare the two men; others simply teach a
lesson or give a rule in the same wise voice. Let the topic decide.

- **Weak man:** makes excuses, follows his mood, wastes money, blames others, runs from hard conversations.
- **Strong man:** keeps his word, follows a routine, builds before he spends, takes responsibility,
  protects and leads his family.
- **Pattern:** "A weak man [does X]. A strong man [does Y]." Always show the weak man first and finish
  on the strong man. The reel should end stronger than it starts.

When the device is not used, keep the same arc: problem or hard truth → turn → what to do → rule.

## 4. Writing a reel script

The voiceover script drives everything (timing, scenes, captions), so get it right first and show it to
the user before building when they have not already approved one.

**Length:** 55–80 words → 25–35 s with pauses. One idea per reel.

**Arc (the approved structure):**

| Beat | Job | Example (approved reel) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook (1 line) | A curiosity gap or hard truth in under 3 s | "The habit no man talks about." |
| Problem / weak man (2 lines) | Name the trap concretely and the cycle it creates | "A weak man gives in every time he is bored, tired or alone. Then he hates himself, and repeats it tomorrow." |
| Turn / strong man (1–3 short lines) | Reframe with a strong image or metaphor | "A strong man knows an urge is a wave. It rises. It passes." |
| Actions (3–4 very short lines) | Concrete, physical, doable steps | "He stands up. He leaves the room. He moves his body. The phone stays outside the bedroom." |
| Rule (1 line) | The quotable law, the line people save | "Control your urges, or they will control you." |
| CTA (1 line) | Consistent sign-off | "Follow for more rules." |

**Rules for lines:**
- Every line is one breath. Prefer 3–8 words, so it can become one scene.
- Concrete nouns beat abstractions ("the phone stays outside the bedroom" beats "limit distractions").
  Concrete nouns also become the reel's objects.
- Repetition is a tool ("It rises. It passes.", "He stands up. He leaves the room.").
- Pauses: `short` after the hook and after each section, `long` before the rule, `hold` after the rule.
- Key words (3–6 per reel) get the accent colour on screen: pick the words that carry the meaning.
- CTA variants: "Follow for more rules." · "Save this. Read it again tomorrow." · "Share this with a man who needs it."

## 5. Topic bank

Mind: discipline vs motivation, the morning rule, phone addiction, comparison, silence, anger control,
keeping your word, overthinking. Body: training when you don't feel like it, sleep, walking, posture,
cold mornings. Money: build before you spend, the first $1,000, debt, buying status, side income,
saying no. Family: protecting your home, being present, hard conversations, leading by example, how you
speak to your partner.

## 6. Safety and monetization guardrails

- Faceless always: no faces, bodies, people or photos. Symbols and objects carry the people.
- No explicit words or imagery. Sensitive topics (for example urges) stay clean and implied.
- No medical, health or financial-return claims ("cures", "guaranteed", "doctors say"). Teach habits, not treatments.
- Never put down women or any group. The weak man is a pattern of behavior, not a type of person.
- Original scripts every time: never copy another creator's lines. Monetization rewards originality.
