# Standing rules for every reel (from the user, 2026-11-01)

These rules apply to every reel for "The Strong Man Code" unless the user says otherwise for a specific
video. They override older rules in this skill where they differ (length, script shape, hook, bottom safe
zone, deliverables). Reason given by the user: views were low because reels felt like lists of quotes. Every
reel is now a short STORY with a PROBLEM and a SOLUTION, and it must stop the scroll in the first second.

## 1. The hook (0-2 s), the most important part
- Frame 0 shows the hook text on screen and an object already moving. No fade from black, no logo, no page
  name, no slow intro. A hard sound hit on frame 0.
- The hook names a real PROBLEM the viewer feels and opens a question he needs answered. Be specific: a time,
  an age, an amount, a moment ("It's 1 AM and you're still scrolling.").
- Hook types (rotate; never the same type twice in a row):
  1. Call out the problem: "If you wake up tired every day, this is why."
  2. Mini story: "He was 29, broke, and still buying new sneakers."
  3. Contrarian: "Motivation is the reason you're still stuck."
  4. The mistake: "Stop doing this with your first paycheck."
  5. The hidden cost: "That habit is costing you more than money."
  6. The secret: "Strong men do one thing every night that weak men skip."
- Never open with "Hey guys", "In this video", the page name, a calm quote with no tension, or a question
  the viewer can answer "no".
- For every reel, write 3 hook options, say why each works in one line, and use the strongest. Record the hook
  type used in prompts/posted-log.md notes so the next reel uses a different type.

## 2. The story shape (every reel, 30-40 s, 75-100 words)
1. HOOK (0-2 s): the problem in one sharp line. It opens a loop.
2. STORY (2-12 s): one man living the problem: a specific moment, what he does, what it costs him. "He" or
   "you". This is the weak side.
3. TURN (12-16 s): one line that flips the story ("Then he learned one rule." / "Here's what nobody told
   him."). The look and sound change here: light, colour or space shifts.
4. SOLUTION (16-30 s): what the strong man does instead, as 3 simple, concrete steps the viewer can do
   today. Number them on screen when it helps.
5. RULE (30-35 s): one line worth screenshotting; the lesson. Slow down here.
6. CTA (last 2-4 s): short and fitted to the story ("Follow if you're done being him." / "Save this for
   tomorrow morning."). When possible, echo the hook so the reel loops. No engagement bait ("comment YES").

Example of the shape (structure only, never reuse the words):
"He said yes to everyone. Then he had nothing left for himself. Every favor. Every late shift. Every 'just
this once.' By thirty, he was tired, broke, and still called the reliable one. Then an old man told him one
thing: your yes is worth nothing if you can't say no. So he started. No to the shift that stole his sleep. No
to the friend who only called for money. Yes to his family, his body, his work. Guard your yes. It's the most
expensive thing you own. Follow if you're learning to say no."

## 3. Script voice and content
- A wise, charismatic older man: calm, direct, certain, a little cold. Short heavy sentences.
- US spelling, simple English that works in the UK and the USA. Always original.
- No jokes, slang, shouting, begging for likes, or putting down women or any group.
- No medical or financial claims, no invented statistics presented as facts, nothing against Islam (no
  alcohol, gambling, dating, interest shown as good, immodesty).
- Every sentence must move the story. If a line doesn't, cut it.

## 4. Pacing and retention
- A visual change every 1.5-2.5 s: a new object, a camera move, a layout change or a cut.
- The story moves fast; the rule slows down and lands with weight.
- Keep one question open until the solution. Tease the fix early ("the fix takes 2 minutes") and pay it off.
- Captions are big, placed high in the frame and readable on mute. Nothing important in the bottom 500 px
  (Facebook's buttons and caption cover it): text and key objects stay between y 120 and y 1420.

## 5. Look and motion
- 1080x1920, 30 fps, HyperFrames. Faceless: no people, faces, bodies or photos. No emojis. Words plus
  symbolic objects only.
- Dark, high-contrast canvas, ONE accent colour per reel (new every time), warm off-white text, a
  desaturated tone for the problem side.
- A bold condensed display face plus a contrasting face for small words; self-hosted fonts.
- Life layer: grain or texture, breathing vignette, thin progress line. No headers, tags, timers or labels.
- Hyper-motion: no static frames, layered parallax, slow push-ins, at least 3 different eases per scene,
  staggered words, objects that wind up and settle. Nothing linear.
- A new visual pair for the problem side and the solution side every reel. One concrete object per scene
  that ACTS on the spoken word. A different text layout every scene.
- Every spoken word appears on screen exactly when it is spoken.

## 6. Transitions and sound
- One signature transition (about 60% of cuts) plus 3-5 accent transitions chosen for meaning: the hook
  landing, the cost of the problem, the TURN, each solution step, the rule.
- Deep, wise, masculine narrator (ElevenLabs Bill Adams, estimate the cost first; otherwise the local voice),
  one take. Slightly faster in the story, slower on the rule.
- Hard hit on frame 0, a whoosh on every cut, impacts on the hard moments, a clear sound shift on the TURN,
  a low hit under the rule, a low bed, everything ducked under the voice (keep the house rule: smooth,
  never harsh or hissy).

## 7. Never copy past reels
- Check video-projects/ (and the reel list in this skill) before designing. Never reuse the symbols,
  objects, colours, fonts, layouts or transitions of an earlier reel, including the skill's examples.
- Use this skill only for its pipeline: voice, word timing, sound mix, build, lint, render and checks.

## 8. Ending
- Hold still on the rule, a small emblem of the solution symbol, the CTA and "THE STRONG MAN CODE",
  readable as a thumbnail.

## 9. Workflow for every reel
1. Before building, show: 3 hook options and the pick, the full script marked HOOK / STORY / TURN /
   SOLUTION / RULE / CTA with word count and estimated length, a one-paragraph creative concept, and a scene
   table (line -> object -> layout -> transition -> sound). Wait for the OK unless told to go straight ahead.
2. Build and render at high quality (HyperFrames --quality high, H.264, CRF 18).
3. Check the real MP4, not only previews:
   - the first frame shows the hook clearly and works as a thumbnail
   - every word matches the voice and lands on time
   - no overlaps, no text outside the safe margins (90 px sides, 120 px top, 500 px bottom)
   - nothing static for more than 2.5 s
4. Deliver: the high-quality MP4, a share copy under 30 MB, a thumbnail made from the hook frame (frame 0),
   and the scene timings. (This replaces the older "HQ only, no share copy or thumbnail" rule.) Keep
   delivering the Facebook description + first comment with every reel and logging it.
